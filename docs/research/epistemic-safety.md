# Epistemic-safety failure record

A private, frozen eight-family holdout rejected the hypothesis that plain Memory3D retrieval was
sufficient for the evaluated context-assembly policy. In one severe Memory3D case, a Kafka-only rule
was admitted under that policy for a filesystem request. The expected behavior was abstention with an
out-of-scope disposition. That failure blocked promotion regardless of average task quality.

The same campaign found missing conflict or supersession status, incomplete provenance, and
unsupported assertions. It did not establish model-independent safety: it covered one quantized
local executor, synthetic scenarios, and three repetitions. The result is nevertheless strong
enough to reject silent promotion of retrieved text as authoritative context.

The failure motivated a bounded evidence envelope with source and producer identity, scope,
lifecycle state, conflict and supersession references, decision provenance, and reconstructable
derivation. Admission remains a query-policy decision over caller-supplied provenance and status;
the core does not infer truth or authority.

Private source commit `f015317f2936fdf735ffb00f4c256c57e2661fc2` records the completed
experiment. The hash is provenance only and does not imply public Git ancestry. Raw campaign
material and execution infrastructure are deliberately not included here.
